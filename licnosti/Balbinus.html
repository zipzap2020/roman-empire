Balbinus: The Aristocratic Compromise

Decimus Caelius Calvinus Balbinus was the civilian counterpart to Pupienus during the desperate Year of the Six Emperors in 238 AD. A man of noble birth, immense wealth, and noted oratorical skill, he represented the traditional Roman elite. While Pupienus was the hardened veteran sent to fight on the front lines, Balbinus remained in Rome to manage the administration and the Senate. His brief reign was a symbol of the Senate's last serious attempt to assert its authority over the military, but it ultimately proved that prestige and pedigree were no match for the swords of the Praetorian Guard.

The Reluctant Ruler

Balbinus was a seasoned politician who had served as consul twice and governed several provinces. When the two Gordians fell in Africa, the Senate met in secret in the Temple of Jupiter to elect successors who could stand against Maximinus Thrax. They chose Balbinus for his popularity within the city and his perceived steadiness.

However, the Roman public was not easily satisfied. They viewed Balbinus and Pupienus as a self serving senatorial committee. During their inauguration, a mob blocked their path to the Capitol, demanding a ruler with a connection to the beloved Gordian family. It was only after the emperors agreed to adopt the young Gordian III as their heir that the rioting subsided enough for them to begin their work.

A House Divided Against Itself

While Pupienus was away in northern Italy managing the war, Balbinus struggled to maintain order in a city on the edge of anarchy. Blood was shed in the streets of Rome between the gladiators and the city cohorts, and a massive fire broke out that destroyed large sections of the capital. Balbinus, though a skilled speaker, found himself unable to control the chaos.

When Pupienus returned victorious, the two men’s mutual suspicion turned into a paralysis of government. They lived in different wings of the palace, each surrounded by their own German bodyguards. Balbinus feared that Pupienus wanted to use his military prestige to become sole ruler, while Pupienus viewed Balbinus as a weak link who was losing control of the city.

The Bloody New Year

The end came during the Capitoline Games in July 238 AD. The Praetorian Guard, resenting the Senate's attempt to pick the emperor without their consent, stormed the imperial residence. In a final, bitter irony, the two emperors were arguing in the hallway about which guards to trust when the assassins broke through the doors.

The guards seized both men, mocked their high titles, and dragged them toward the Praetorian camp. They were killed before they reached the gates, their bodies left in the street to be defiled by the mob. With their deaths, the Senate’s short-lived experiment in dual civilian-military rule vanished, leaving the thirteen year old Gordian III as the last man standing in a world ruled by the spear.
