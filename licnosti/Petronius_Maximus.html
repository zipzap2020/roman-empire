Petronius Maximus: The Brief and Violent Rise of a Roman Senator

Petronius Maximus was a wealthy and influential aristocrat who managed to reach the highest office in the Roman state, but only for a very short and chaotic period. Born into a prominent family around 397, he spent decades climbing the political ladder, serving as a consul and a high ranking administrator. However, his ambition eventually led him into a series of conspiracies that would trigger one of the most famous disasters in the history of the city of Rome.

The Path of Betrayal

The rise of Maximus to the throne was paved with blood. He played a key role in the plot to turn Emperor Valentinian III against his most successful general, Aetius. Once Aetius was removed and murdered, the empire lost its main protector, but Maximus was still not satisfied. He wanted the throne for himself, and shortly after the death of the general, he orchestrated the assassination of Valentinian III as well. With the emperor dead and no clear successor, Maximus used his wealth and connections in the Senate to secure the crown for himself in March 455.

To strengthen his weak claim, he forced the widow of the murdered emperor to marry him and tried to tie his own son to the royal bloodline. These actions backfired completely. By breaking previous diplomatic agreements and forcing these marriages, he deeply offended Geiseric, the powerful King of the Vandals. When the news reached Rome that a Vandal fleet was sailing toward Italy, the city was gripped by panic. Maximus, realizing he had no army to defend himself, tried to flee rather than stay and fight.

A Brutal End and the Sack of Rome

His reign ended just seventy five days after it began. As he tried to escape the city on horseback, he was recognized by an angry mob of Roman citizens. They were furious that their leader was abandoning them in their moment of greatest need, and they attacked him with stones. Maximus was killed in the street, and his body was mutilated before being thrown into the Tiber river.

Just three days after his death, the Vandals arrived and began a systematic looting of Rome that lasted for two weeks. While the city survived, the damage to its wealth and prestige was enormous. Petronius Maximus is remembered today as a man who allowed his personal ambition to outweigh the safety of the empire, ultimately leading to a disaster that accelerated the final collapse of the Western Roman world.
