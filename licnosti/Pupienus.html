Pupienus: The Senate's Iron Soldier

Marcus Clodius Pupienus Maximus was one half of the most unusual leadership duos in Roman history. Following the tragic deaths of the two Gordians in Africa, the Senate was left in a state of sheer terror. Maximinus Thrax was still marching toward Rome with fire in his eyes. In a final, desperate act of defiance, the Senate elected two of their own to rule as joint emperors in 238 AD: Pupienus and Balbinus. While Balbinus was the wealthy orator, Pupienus was the grit and the muscle. A man of humble origins who had clawed his way up through the military, he was chosen for one reason: to stop the "Giant" at the gates of Italy.

A Partnership of Mutual Hatred

The choice of Pupienus and Balbinus was a political compromise that nearly failed before it began. The two men famously loathed each other. Pupienus viewed Balbinus as a soft, pampered aristocrat, while Balbinus looked down on Pupienus as a harsh and unrefined soldier.

The Roman people weren't thrilled either. They rioted in the streets, demanding a ruler from the bloodline of the fallen Gordians. To appease the mob, the two emperors were forced to name the young grandson of Gordian I—the future Gordian III—as their Caesar and heir. With a shaky peace established in the capital, Pupienus left Rome to face the approaching army of Maximinus Thrax.

The Defense of Aquileia

Pupienus proved to be a brilliant strategist. Instead of meeting Maximinus in a pitched battle, he organized the defense of northern Italy, turning the city of Aquileia into an impenetrable fortress. He ordered the surrounding countryside to be stripped of all food and supplies, leaving the invading army to starve.

As Maximinus’s soldiers grew tired of the endless siege and the lack of rations, they eventually turned on their own leader and murdered him. When the heads of Maximinus and his son were sent to Pupienus, he didn't gloat. He sent the remains to Rome and paid off the rebel soldiers, successfully ending the immediate threat of civil war without a massive clash of legions.

Assassination in the Palace

When Pupienus returned to Rome as a hero, the friction between the two emperors became fatal. They spent their days arguing in the palace, each suspecting the other of plotting an assassination. This internal bickering left them vulnerable to the Praetorian Guard, who felt sidelined by these "Senate emperors."

In July 238 AD, only ninety nine days into their reign, the Praetorians stormed the palace. While Pupienus and Balbinus were busy accusing each other of treachery, the guards seized them, stripped them, and dragged them through the streets of Rome. Both men were murdered, leaving the young boy Gordian III as the sole ruler of a traumatized empire.
