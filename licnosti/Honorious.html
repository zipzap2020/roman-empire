Honorius and the Twilight of the Western Empire

History often remembers Flavius Honorius as a ruler who was simply out of his depth. He became the emperor of the West when he was only nine years old after his father Theodosius died. Because he was so young, he didn't actually hold the power. Instead, the fate of Rome was mostly in the hands of his generals and advisors during those early years.

The Struggle for Survival

The most important figure in the first half of his reign was a general named Stilicho. For a long time, Stilicho was the only thing standing between the empire and the various tribes trying to cross the borders. Things took a turn for the worse when Honorius was convinced to turn against his general. After he had Stilicho executed, the empire lost its best protector and the path to Rome was left wide open for invaders.

In the year 410, the unthinkable happened. A group called the Visigoths led by Alaric managed to break into Rome and spend three days taking whatever they wanted. Honorius wasn't even in the city at the time because he had moved his court to Ravenna. He chose that city because it was surrounded by swamps and much easier to defend than Rome, but his absence during the crisis made him look weak to his people.

A Strange Legacy

There is a well known story that summarizes his reputation quite well. Supposedly, when an official rushed in to tell him that Rome had been destroyed, Honorius was shocked because he thought the man was talking about his favorite pet chicken which was also named Rome. When he realized the city was what they meant, he was reportedly relieved that his bird was still alive. Even if the story isn't true, it shows how people felt about his lack of leadership.

By the time he died in 423, the empire was a shadow of its former self. He lost control over Britain and large parts of Gaul and Spain. Honorius might not have been a cruel man, but his inability to lead during a time of crisis definitely speeded up the collapse of the Roman world.
