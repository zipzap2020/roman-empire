Pertinax: The Eighty-Seven Day Emperor

Publius Helvius Pertinax was the first emperor of the turbulent Year of the Five Emperors. A self made man, he was born the son of a freed slave and rose through the ranks as a teacher and then a brilliant military officer. When Commodus was strangled on New Year’s Eve, the conspirators turned to the elderly, respected Pertinax to restore order. His reign in early 193 AD was a brief, tragic attempt to bring back the discipline of the age of Marcus Aurelius to a city that had become accustomed to excess.

The Reluctant Reformer

Pertinax was 66 years old when he was woken in the middle of the night and told he was the new master of the Roman world. He immediately set to work trying to fix the mess Commodus had left behind. He auctioned off the former emperor's lavish belongings—including his silk robes and gold plated chariots—to refill the empty treasury. He also attempted to reform the grain supply and improve land use, earning him the respect of the Senate and the common people.

However, his commitment to "Old Rome" discipline proved to be his undoing. He inherited an imperial guard that had been spoiled by the laxity and bribes of the previous decade. When Pertinax refused to pay the full, massive bonus the soldiers expected and insisted they return to strict military standards, the Praetorian Guard realized they preferred a puppet to a schoolmaster.

The Palace Mutiny

On March 28, 193 AD, only eighty seven days into his rule, a group of nearly three hundred angry Praetorian soldiers rushed the gates of the palace. While his advisors urged him to flee, Pertinax chose to face the men directly. He hoped that his gray hair, his dignity, and his status as a former comrade in arms would shame them into standing down.

For a moment, it worked. The soldiers reportedly wavered as he spoke to them. But then, a Tungrian soldier struck him with a spear, and the rest of the mob joined in. Pertinax was killed as he covered his head with his toga, praying to the gods.

The Empire for Sale

The death of Pertinax led to one of the most shameful moments in Roman history. With the throne vacant, the Praetorian Guard did not look for a leader or a hero; they decided to sell the Roman Empire to the highest bidder. They stood on the walls of their camp and literally auctioned off the title of Emperor.

A wealthy senator named Didius Julianus eventually outbid everyone else, promising each soldier a fortune. This act of blatant corruption horrified the Roman world and sparked immediate revolts across the provinces, as the great generals on the frontiers prepared to march on Rome to avenge Pertinax and claim the throne for themselves.
