Carausius

Mausaeus Carausius was a Roman military commander of humble origins who achieved one of the most remarkable feats of the third century. A member of the Menapii, a Belgic tribe, he rose through the ranks of the Roman navy to become a key figure in the defense of the empire. However, rather than remaining a loyal servant, he seized the opportunity to carve out his own sovereign state, ruling as a self proclaimed emperor in Britain and northern Gaul for seven years.

From Admiral to Usurper

In 286, Carausius was given a vital mission by the Emperor Maximian: he was to command the Classis Britannica, the Roman fleet based in the English Channel, with the goal of eliminating Frankish and Saxon pirates. While he was highly successful in battle, rumors soon reached the imperial court that Carausius was allowing the pirates to carry out their raids and then capturing them only to keep the recovered treasure for himself.

When Maximian ordered his execution, Carausius responded with a bold rebellion. He crossed the Channel to Britain, won over the local legions, and declared himself Emperor. He proved to be a savvy ruler, minting high quality silver coins that featured his portrait alongside those of the legitimate emperors Diocletian and Maximian, styling them as his brothers in an attempt to gain recognition. This era is often referred to as the Carausian Revolt, which effectively created a breakaway "British Empire."

The Shore Defenses and a Betrayed End

To protect his realm, Carausius developed a sophisticated system of coastal fortifications known as the Saxon Shore forts. These massive stone strongholds were designed to repel both pirate raids and any invasion attempt by the legitimate Roman authorities. For several years, he successfully held his territory, even defeating a naval invasion launched by Maximian in 289.

However, his luck ran out when the Caesar Constantius Chlorus launched a new campaign in 293. Constantius successfully besieged Carausius's main continental base at Gesoriacum (modern day Boulogne). This military failure weakened Carausius’s standing among his own men. Shortly after, he was assassinated by his treasurer, Allectus, who then took the throne for himself. Although his reign ended in murder, Carausius is remembered as a visionary commander who turned Britain into a maritime power long before the rise of the modern British navy.
