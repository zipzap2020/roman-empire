Trebonianus Gallus: Peace at Any Price

Gaius Vibius Trebonianus Gallus ascended to the throne in one of the darkest hours of Roman history. In 251 AD, following the total destruction of the Roman army at the Battle of Abritus and the death of Emperor Decius, Gallus was proclaimed emperor by the surviving, demoralized troops. His two year reign was a desperate exercise in damage control as he tried to juggle a humiliating peace treaty, a devastating pandemic, and a crumbling economy. While he was a capable administrator, he could not overcome the perception that he was a weak ruler who had bought safety with Roman dignity.

The Shameful Peace

The first act of Gallus’s reign made him deeply unpopular. To get the victorious Goths to leave Roman territory, he agreed to a treaty that allowed them to keep all the loot and prisoners they had captured. Even worse, he promised to pay the Goths an annual subsidy in gold.

To the citizens of Rome, this looked like the empire was paying "barbarian rent." Although Gallus likely did this to buy time to rebuild the shattered legions, it branded him as a coward in the eyes of the military. He hurried back to Rome to secure his political position, adopting Decius’s surviving son, Hostilian, to bridge the gap between the old administration and his own.

A Reign Defined by Death

Gallus’s time in Rome was dominated by the Plague of Cyprian. As the disease slaughtered thousands, Gallus earned some respect for organizing the burial of the poor and providing relief to the suffering. However, the plague also provided a convenient excuse for renewed tension with the Christians. Many Romans blamed the "atheism" of the Christians for angering the gods, and Gallus allowed localized persecutions to continue, most notably the exile of Pope Cornelius.

While the plague raged in the west, the Sassanid Persians under Shapur I took advantage of the chaos to invade Syria, reportedly destroying a Roman army of 60,000 men and sacking the great city of Antioch. Gallus was paralyzed; he couldn't leave Italy because of the plague and the threat of usurpers, but he couldn't protect the east from his palace in Rome.

The Rise of Aemilian

The end came from the same region where Gallus had first been crowned. Aemilian, the governor of Moesia, refused to pay the tribute to the Goths and instead led a successful counterattack against them. His soldiers, thrilled by a victory for once, proclaimed him emperor.

Aemilian marched on Italy with incredible speed. Gallus and his son Volusianus gathered what few troops they had and moved to meet him. However, when the two armies faced off near Interamna, Gallus’s own soldiers realized they were outnumbered and outmatched. Rather than die for a man they didn't respect, they murdered Gallus and Volusianus in August 253 AD and went over to the side of the winner.
