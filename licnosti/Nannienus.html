Nannienus

Nannienus was a distinguished military commander of the late fourth century who served under the Western Roman Emperor Gratian. As a high-ranking officer, he played a crucial role in stabilizing the northern frontiers of the empire during a period of intense pressure from Germanic tribes. He is often remembered for his disciplined leadership and his ability to coordinate complex defensive operations alongside other notable generals of the era.

The Battle of Argentovaria

One of the most significant moments in his career occurred in 378. While Emperor Gratian was preparing to move east to assist his uncle Valens against the Goths, the Lentienses, a branch of the Alamanni, took advantage of the situation and crossed the Rhine. Nannienus was tasked with holding the line. He shared command with Mallobaudes, a Frankish king who served in the Roman army as a general.

The two leaders met the invading forces at the Battle of Argentovaria. Nannienus advocated for a more cautious and tactical approach, ensuring the Roman lines remained firm against the initial barbarian charge. The strategy was a resounding success. The Roman forces achieved a devastating victory, reportedly killing the Lentienses' king and the vast majority of their warriors. This triumph secured the Rhine frontier and allowed Gratian to continue his march toward the Eastern Empire.

Legacy and Authority

Nannienus continued to hold high office after this victory, eventually being appointed as a commander in Gaul. His tenure was marked by the difficult task of managing the internal politics of the Roman military while simultaneously fending off external threats. Historical accounts by Sulpicius Alexander mention his later activities alongside the general Quintinus, as they attempted to manage the fallout from Frankish raids during the reign of the usurper Magnus Maximus.

His career reflects the classic Roman military tradition of the late fourth century: a reliance on professional discipline, heavy infantry tactics, and a willingness to work with barbarian allies to protect the borders. Though less famous than some of his successors, Nannienus was instrumental in providing the Western Empire with a few more decades of security along the Rhine.
