Vitus

Vitus was a high ranking Roman general who served as Magister Militum during the mid fifth century. His career unfolded at a time when the Western Roman Empire was desperately trying to maintain its grip on the Iberian Peninsula. He is primarily known for leading a significant but ultimately disastrous military expedition aimed at curbing the power of the Suebi, a Germanic tribe that had established a kingdom in what is now modern day Spain and Portugal.

The Spanish Expedition

In 446, Vitus was dispatched to Hispania with a sizable force, supported by a large contingent of Gothic auxiliaries. At this time, the Suebi, under King Rechila, had been aggressively expanding their territory at the expense of Roman administration. The mission of Vitus was a clear attempt by the central government in Ravenna to reassert imperial authority and protect the remaining Roman provinces in the region.

The campaign initially saw the Roman forces moving through the provinces of Carthaginiensis and Baetica. However, the situation quickly turned dire. When the Roman army encountered the Suebi forces, the Gothic allies who were supposed to bolster the Roman ranks reportedly became terrified or unwilling to fight. This led to a complete collapse of the Roman position. The Suebi defeated Vitus's army decisively, and the general was forced to flee the region in disgrace.

The Aftermath of Defeat

The failure of Vitus had long lasting consequences for the Roman presence in Spain. Following his defeat, the Suebi were able to plunder the wealthy southern provinces almost at will, further draining the empire of resources and tax revenue. The loss of prestige and military strength during this campaign signaled that the Western Empire no longer had the capacity to police its more distant territories effectively.

Historically, Vitus represents the struggle of the late Roman military elite to manage a "shadow empire" where the real power often lay with the barbarian tribes they were forced to employ as mercenaries. His defeat was one of the many steps that led to the eventual total loss of Hispania, as the empire turned its focus inward to protect the Italian heartland.
