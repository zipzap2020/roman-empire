Trajan: The Optimus Princeps of the Roman Empire

Trajan, born Marcus Ulpius Traianus, was the first Roman Emperor born outside of Italy, hailing from Italica in modern day Spain. His reign from 98 to 117 AD is often cited by historians as the pinnacle of the Roman Empire. He was officially declared by the Senate as "Optimus Princeps," or the Best Ruler, a title he earned through a rare combination of military brilliance, massive public building projects, and a genuine concern for the welfare of the common citizen.

The Conquest of Dacia

Trajan’s military reputation was cemented by his two wars against the Dacian Kingdom, located in what is now Romania. King Decebalus had long been a thorn in Rome's side, but Trajan led a massive force across the Danube to finally end the threat. He oversaw incredible engineering feats during this campaign, including the construction of a massive bridge over the Danube, which was the longest arch bridge in the world for over a thousand years.

The victory was total. Dacia was converted into a Roman province, and the enormous gold and silver mines of the region flooded the Roman treasury. This wealth funded the most ambitious building program in Rome’s history. To commemorate his victory, he built Trajan’s Column, a masterpiece of ancient art that features a continuous spiral relief depicting the entire story of the Dacian Wars.

Expansion to the East

Not content with European conquests, Trajan turned his attention to the Parthian Empire in the East. He marched his legions through Armenia and Mesopotamia, eventually capturing the Parthian capital of Ctesiphon. He even reached the Persian Gulf, where he famously lamented that he was too old to follow in the footsteps of Alexander the Great.

Under his leadership, the Roman Empire reached its greatest territorial extent. From the rainy hills of Britain to the deserts of the Persian Gulf, the Roman world had never been larger or more secure.

The Architect of the People

Trajan was just as active at home as he was on the battlefield. He built Trajan's Forum, a massive complex that included the famous Trajan's Market, often called the world's first shopping mall. He also established the "Alimenta," a welfare program that provided subsidized food and education to the poor children of Italy.

Unlike the paranoid tyrants who had preceded him, Trajan lived simply and walked the streets of Rome without a massive guard, earning the genuine love of the people. When he died in 117 AD, his ashes were placed in the base of his great column, a unique honor for an emperor. For centuries after his death, new emperors were inaugurated with the wish that they be "luckier than Augustus and better than Trajan."
