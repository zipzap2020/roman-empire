Galba

Galba: The First of the Four

Servius Sulpicius Galba was the man who stepped into the power vacuum left by Nero's suicide. As the governor of Hispania Tarraconensis, he was already an old man of seventy one when he was proclaimed emperor. He represented a return to the old school Roman virtues: he was stern, frugal, and strictly disciplined. However, the qualities that made him a respected general in his youth made him a disastrous politician in his old age. His brief seven month reign in 68 to 69 AD proved that the prestige of the aristocracy was no longer enough to control an empire that now belonged to the soldiers.

The March to Rome

Galba’s path to the throne was cleared by the rebellion of Vindex in Gaul and the desertion of the Praetorian Guard from Nero. When he finally arrived in Rome, the city was in a state of nervous expectation. Unlike the lavish and generous Nero, Galba immediately began a program of extreme austerity. He attempted to claw back the massive gifts Nero had given to courtiers and performers, which made him enemies in every corner of the palace.

His biggest mistake, however, was his treatment of the military. He famously stated that he "chose his soldiers and did not buy them." By refusing to pay the standard "donative" or bonus to the Praetorian Guard and the legions that had supported his rise, he stripped away the only real foundation of his power.

The German Revolt

While Galba struggled to manage the politics of Rome, the situation on the frontiers was deteriorating. The legions in Germania Inferior refused to renew their oath of allegiance to him on New Year's Day, 69 AD. Instead, they threw down his statues and proclaimed their own commander, Vitellius, as emperor.

In a desperate attempt to stabilize his government and show he had a future, Galba adopted a young aristocrat named Piso Licinianus as his son and successor. This was a move intended to follow the noble precedents of Augustus, but it backfired completely by insulting Marcus Salvius Otho, a former supporter of Galba who had expected to be the heir himself.

Death in the Forum

Otho did not wait for the German legions to arrive. He turned to the disgruntled Praetorian Guard and bribed them with the money Galba had refused to pay. On January 15, 69 AD, while Galba was offering a sacrifice in the Forum, the soldiers struck.

Galba was carried through the streets in a litter, but when the rebelling cavalry appeared, his bearers fled. He was thrown to the ground and killed near the Lacus Curtius. His head was cut off and paraded on a spear, and his body was left to be defiled by the mob. With his death, the "Year of the Four Emperors" entered its most violent phase, as the empire prepared for a collision between the forces of Otho in Rome and Vitellius on the march from the Rhine.
