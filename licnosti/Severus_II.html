Severus II

Severus II: The Fallen Choice

Flavius Valerius Severus, commonly known as Severus II, was a soldier of humble Illyrian origins who became a victim of the very system intended to protect him. Elevated to the rank of Caesar in 305 AD during the grand reshuffling of the Tetrarchy, he was the hand picked choice of Galerius. Severus was a loyal officer and a reliable friend, but he lacked the political weight and the raw charisma needed to survive the explosion of civil wars that followed the death of Constantius Chlorus. His reign was a brief, tragic struggle to maintain an "official" legitimacy that the soldiers in the street no longer respected.

A Promotion into Chaos

When Diocletian and Maximian retired, Severus was named the junior emperor (Caesar) of the West, serving under Constantius Chlorus. However, the real power dynamic was skewed: Severus was a creature of the Eastern Emperor Galerius, intended to keep a watchful eye on the Western territories.

The system broke almost immediately in 306 AD. When Constantius died in Britain, the army ignored Severus and proclaimed Constantine as emperor. To make matters worse, the city of Rome, feeling neglected by the new administrative capitals, rose up and proclaimed Maximian’s son, Maxentius, as their leader. Galerius promoted Severus to the full rank of Augustus and ordered him to march on Rome to crush the usurper.

The Siege of the Eternal City

Severus arrived at the gates of Rome with a massive army, many of whom had previously served under the old Emperor Maximian. This proved to be his undoing. Maxentius used his father’s name and heavy bribes to subvert the loyalty of Severus’s troops.

As Severus began the siege, he watched in horror as his own soldiers deserted in mass to join the "son of their old commander." Fearing he would be handed over by his remaining guards, Severus fled to the fortified city of Ravenna. It was a tactical retreat that turned into a trap.

The Promise of Ravenna

Maximian himself, having come out of retirement, arrived at Ravenna to negotiate. He promised Severus that if he surrendered and abdicated the throne, his life would be spared and he would be allowed to live in peaceful retirement. Severus, exhausted and without an army, took the deal in 307 AD.

The promise was a lie. Severus was taken as a prisoner to a villa near Rome, held as a political hostage against Galerius. When Galerius eventually invaded Italy to rescue his friend, Maxentius decided that Severus was too dangerous to keep alive. Under mysterious circumstances, the captive emperor was forced to "open his veins," or was simply murdered, ending his life in the same city he had failed to conquer.

The End of the Official Line

The death of Severus II signaled that the Tetrarchy’s rules for promotion were effectively dead. Power was no longer something granted by a senior emperor in a boardroom; it was something seized by sons and generals on the battlefield. His failure cleared the path for the long, bloody rivalry between Maxentius and Constantine.
