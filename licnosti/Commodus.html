Commodus: The Gladiator Emperor

Lucius Aurelius Commodus was the man who broke Rome’s golden age. As the first emperor in over a century to inherit the throne from his biological father, Marcus Aurelius, he faced immense expectations. However, Commodus had no interest in the Stoic duty or administrative rigor of his father. His reign from 180 to 192 AD was a descent into megalomania, where the Emperor abandoned the halls of government for the bloody sands of the arena. His rule ended the Nerva-Antonine dynasty and plunged the empire into a century of instability.

The End of the Northern Wars

One of the first acts Commodus performed was also his most controversial. Rather than continuing his father’s grueling campaigns against the Germanic tribes, he quickly negotiated peace treaties and returned to the luxuries of Rome. While some modern historians view this as a pragmatic move to save money and lives, the Roman elite saw it as a cowardly abandonment of his father’s legacy.

Upon his return, Commodus left the management of the empire to a series of powerful favorites, such as Saoterus, Perennis, and later the freedman Cleander. This allowed the Emperor to focus entirely on his own pleasures while his ministers grew wealthy and executed their political rivals, fueling a culture of deep seated paranoia in the imperial court.

Hercules Reborn

As the years passed, Commodus became convinced he was the reincarnation of the mythical hero Hercules. He began appearing in public wearing a lion skin and carrying a club. He even went as far as to rename the city of Rome "Colonia Commodiana" and changed the names of the twelve months to match his various titles.

His most shocking behavior was his obsession with the gladiatorial games. Commodus actually entered the arena to fight as a gladiator, an act considered utterly shameful for a Roman citizen. While he always "won" (often because his opponents were handicapped or terrified to strike back), he charged the city of Rome a massive fee for every appearance, further draining the already struggling treasury.

The Conspiracy of 192

The final straw came when Commodus announced he intended to begin his new consulship in 193 AD dressed as a gladiator rather than in traditional robes. His inner circle—including his mistress Marcia, his chamberlain, and the prefect of the Praetorian Guard—realized the Emperor was beyond reason and that their lives were in danger.

On New Year's Eve, 192 AD, Marcia attempted to poison his wine. When the Emperor simply vomited the poison and went to take a bath, the conspirators sent in his wrestling partner, a man named Narcissus, to strangle him. Commodus died at age thirty one. Like Domitian before him, the Senate declared "Damnatio Memoriae" on his name. His death triggered a violent civil war known as the "Year of the Five Emperors," as the stability his father had fought so hard for finally crumbled.
