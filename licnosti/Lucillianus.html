Lucillianus: The Commander of Two Frontiers

Service in the East and West

Lucillianus first gained prominence during the reign of Constantius II. In 350, he successfully defended the strategic city of Nisibis against a massive siege by the Sassanid Persian King Shapur II. His ability to hold the fortress against overwhelming odds proved his tactical worth. Later, he was sent on a delicate diplomatic mission to the Persian court, showing that he was trusted with the empire's most sensitive foreign relations.

In the West, however, his luck was more varied. When the young Caesar Julian rose in rebellion against Constantius II, Lucillianus remained loyal to the reigning emperor. He was stationed in Sirmium to guard the approach to the Balkans. In a daring night raid, Julian’s elite troops captured Lucillianus while he was still asleep. When he was brought before the rebel leader and urged to show submission, Julian reportedly mocked the old general’s warnings, telling him to save his advice for someone who needed it.

The Final Rebellion

After the death of Julian in the East, Lucillianus’s son in law, Jovian, was proclaimed emperor. This sudden shift in fortune brought Lucillianus back into the highest circles of power. He was sent to Rheims in Gaul to secure the loyalty of the western armies. This proved to be a fatal assignment.

Upon arriving, he discovered that a subordinate had been embezzling military funds. When Lucillianus attempted to punish the corrupt official, the man stirred up a riot among the soldiers by falsely claiming that Julian was still alive and that Lucillianus was a traitor. The enraged troops attacked the general’s quarters and murdered him in 363. Despite his long years of service to the Roman state, his life ended at the hands of the very men he was sent to lead, illustrating the extreme volatility of the late Roman military.
