Didius Julianus

Didius Julianus: The Highest Bidder

Didius Julianus holds the unenviable distinction of being the man who bought the Roman Empire at an auction. His reign in 193 AD lasted only sixty six days and is remembered as one of the most pathetic and desperate episodes in the long history of the city. A wealthy senator who had served as a governor in several provinces, Julianus was not a villain, but a man of immense vanity who mistook his bank account for a mandate to rule.

The Auction of the World

After the murder of Pertinax, the Praetorian Guard realized they held the ultimate power. They stood on the ramparts of their fortified camp and shouted to the city that the throne was for sale to whoever offered the largest donative. Two men emerged to bid: Sulpicianus, the father in law of the murdered Pertinax, and Didius Julianus.

As the two men shouted their offers from outside the walls, the price climbed higher and higher. Julianus eventually made a massive leap in the bidding, promising 25,000 sesterces to every single soldier in the Guard. The soldiers opened the gates, hailed him as Emperor, and escorted him to the Senate house.

A City in Mourning

The Roman people were horrified. When Julianus walked to the Senate, the crowds did not cheer; they hissed and threw stones, shouting that he had stolen the empire. Julianus tried to win them over with promises of games and money, but he was seen as a puppet of the Praetorians.

The news of the auction traveled quickly to the frontiers. Three major generals—Septimius Severus in Pannonia, Pescennius Niger in Syria, and Clodius Albinus in Britain—refused to recognize a ruler who had bought his way to power. They all declared themselves emperor and began their march toward Italy. Severus, being the closest to Rome, moved with terrifying speed.

The Desertion and the End

As Septimius Severus crossed the Alps, the panic in Rome reached a fever pitch. Julianus tried to prepare the city for a siege, even attempting to train the palace elephants for war, but the soldiers were disorganized and lazy. Desperate, Julianus offered to share the throne with Severus, but the general ignored him.

Realizing that Severus was unstoppable, the Senate and the Praetorian Guard abandoned Julianus. The Senate sentenced him to death and recognized Severus as the true emperor. Julianus was found in the deserted palace, weeping and alone. As the executioner approached him, his final words were a confused plea: "But what evil have I done? Whom have I killed?" He was executed on June 1, 193 AD.
