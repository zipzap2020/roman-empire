Otho

Otho: The Emperor of the Longest Day

Marcus Salvius Otho was the second figure in the chaotic "Year of the Four Emperors." A former close friend of Nero and the ex-husband of the infamous Poppaea Sabina, Otho was a man of luxury and fashion who surprised everyone with the dignity of his end. His reign lasted only three months in 69 AD, as he found himself caught between the bloody coup that brought him to power and the unstoppable march of the German legions under his rival, Vitellius.

A Coup of Desperation

Otho’s rise was a gamble. After supporting Galba’s rebellion against Nero, he expected to be named heir. When the elderly Galba passed him over for the colorless Piso, Otho turned to the Praetorian Guard. He was deeply in debt and saw the throne as his only escape. On January 15, he orchestrated the assassination of Galba in the Roman Forum and was immediately proclaimed emperor by the soldiers.

While the Senate and the elite viewed him as a "second Nero" due to his flamboyant past, Otho tried to govern with moderation. He restored some of Nero’s statues to please the city’s urban poor and the soldiers, but he also respected the Senate's authority. However, he had no time to settle into his role; the legions of the Rhine had already declared Vitellius emperor and were pouring through the Alps into Italy.

The Battle of Bedriacum

Otho was not a natural soldier, but he showed surprising energy in defending his throne. He gathered what forces he could, including the elite Praetorians and gladiators, and marched north to meet the veterans of the Rhine. The two armies clashed near Cremona at the First Battle of Bedriacum.

The battle was a confused and bloody affair. Otho’s generals were divided on strategy, and his troops, though brave, were inexperienced compared to the battle-hardened German legions. After a day of heavy fighting, Otho’s lines broke. Despite having fresh reinforcements arriving from the Danubian provinces, Otho made a decision that secured his place in the history books for reasons other than tyranny.

The Noble Suicide

Rather than continue a civil war that would bleed Rome dry, Otho decided to end his life to spare the empire further slaughter. He spent his final evening settling his affairs, burning his private correspondence to protect his friends, and distributing his remaining gold to his servants.

At dawn, he took a single dagger and stabbed himself in the heart. His soldiers were so moved by his sacrifice that several of them committed suicide on his funeral pyre. Otho died on April 16, 69 AD, after a reign of just ninety five days. He had lived as a hedonist, but in the eyes of many Romans, he died like a true Stoic, proving that even a flawed man could achieve a "good death."
