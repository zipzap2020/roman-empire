Valentinian III: The Last of a Dynasty

The reign of Valentinian III covers one of the most difficult periods in the history of the Western Roman Empire. He was placed on the throne as a child of only six years old and his time as emperor lasted for thirty years. Because he started so young, the actual work of running the empire was handled by his mother Galla Placidia and later by the powerful general Aetius. Even though he ruled for a long time, his legacy is often overshadowed by the fact that the empire was slowly breaking apart around him.

A World Under Pressure

During his three decades in power, the empire faced constant threats from every direction. The most famous of these challenges came from Attila the Hun, who led his forces deep into Roman territory. It was during this crisis that General Aetius managed to pull together a fragile coalition of Romans and various tribes to stop the Huns at the Battle of the Catalaunian Plains. This was a rare moment of victory in an era that was otherwise filled with the loss of important provinces like North Africa and parts of Spain and Gaul.

As the years went by, the financial strain on the state became unbearable. With fewer territories to tax, the government struggled to pay for the massive armies needed to defend the remaining borders. Valentinian even had to issue new laws to tax the wealthy senators and he even gave up part of his own income to help fund the military. Despite these efforts, the central government in Rome was becoming a shadow of what it once was.

The Violent End of an Era

The final years of his life were marked by internal betrayal. Valentinian grew suspicious of his general Aetius and feared that the man was becoming too powerful. In a sudden fit of rage during a meeting, the emperor himself drew a sword and killed Aetius. It was a decision that backfired almost immediately. By killing the man who held the military together, Valentinian left himself without protection. Just a few months later, he was assassinated by followers of Aetius while he was practicing archery in Rome.

His death marked the end of the Valentinianic dynasty and left the empire in total chaos. Within a very short time, the city of Rome was sacked by the Vandals and the Western Empire moved even closer to its final collapse. He is often remembered as a ruler who lived through the end of an era but lacked the strength to change its course.
