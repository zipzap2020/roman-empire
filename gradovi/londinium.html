The City of London

London During Roman Rule

The Foundation of Londinium

The first major settlement at the site of modern London was founded by the Romans around forty seven AD, shortly after their invasion of Britain. Known as Londinium, it was established as a strategic port on the River Thames. However, this early city was short lived as it was burned to the ground in sixty one AD during the revolt led by Queen Boudica of the Iceni tribe.

The Golden Age of Roman London

After the revolt was suppressed, the Romans rebuilt Londinium with a more organized urban plan. By the year one hundred, it had superseded Colchester as the capital of the province of Britannia. During the second century, the city reached its peak with a population of approximately sixty thousand people. It became a bustling commercial hub with a large forum, public baths, and an amphitheater.

To protect the city, the Romans constructed a massive defensive wall in the late second and early third centuries. Sections of this structure, known as the London Wall, can still be seen today. Despite being far from Rome, the city maintained a high standard of Roman culture and administration for several centuries.

The End of Roman Rule and Abandonment

Roman control over London began to weaken in the fourth century due to internal instability and frequent attacks from outside tribes. When the Roman Empire withdrew its administration from Britain in the early fifth century, the walled city was gradually abandoned. While some remnants of Roman civilization persisted in the surrounding areas, the urban core of Londinium fell into decay, marking the end of the Roman period in the history of the city.
