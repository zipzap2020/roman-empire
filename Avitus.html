Avitus: The Gallic Senator on the Roman Throne

The story of Eparchius Avitus is a unique chapter in the final years of the Western Roman Empire. Unlike many of his predecessors who spent their lives in Italy, Avitus was a nobleman from Gaul, born in a city known today as Clermont. He was a man of high education and legal training who spent much of his early career building bridges between the Roman administration and the powerful Visigoth kingdom. His ability to negotiate with both sides made him a crucial figure during a time when the empire was desperately looking for allies.

A Diplomat Becomes Emperor

Avitus rose to the throne during a moment of extreme crisis. After the violent death of Petronius Maximus and the devastating sack of Rome by the Vandals in 455, the empire was left without a leader. While Avitus was on a diplomatic mission at the court of the Visigoth king Theodoric II, he was acclaimed as emperor by his Gothic friends and later by the Gallic nobility. Although the Roman Senate eventually recognized his authority, he was always seen as a bit of an outsider by the people of Rome.

His reign was marked by an attempt to integrate the Gallic aristocracy more deeply into the imperial government. This policy created a lot of tension with the traditional elite in Italy. At the same time, the city of Rome was suffering from severe food shortages because the Vandals controlled the sea routes. To pay the wages of his soldiers, Avitus was even forced to melt down bronze statues from the city, an act that deeply angered the local population and further weakened his position.

The Fall from Power

The end of his rule came quickly when two powerful military leaders, Ricimer and Majorian, decided to rebel against him. After a bloody battle near Piacenza in 456, Avitus was defeated and forced to give up his crown. Instead of being executed immediately, he was forced to become a bishop in the same city. However, his new life in the church did not last long. Within a year of losing his throne, he died under mysterious circumstances while traveling back toward his home in Gaul.

Avitus represents a final attempt to save the Western Empire through a strong alliance with the provinces and the Germanic tribes. While he was a capable diplomat and a brave soldier, he could not overcome the internal divisions and the economic collapse that were tearing the Roman world apart. His death cleared the way for Majorian to take the throne, but the dream of a united Western Empire was becoming harder to maintain with each passing year.
